# Wander: close views of regional eras

Generated on 25 September 2026 with the built-in ImageGen tool.

The brief: zoom farther into the world and explore the Bronze Age and a specific
period in Asia. This set uses the Aegean Bronze Age, Tang China, and Edo Japan
to explore how both place and time can determine the terrain's material language.

| Display order | Setting | Image | Material direction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Northern Crete and Knossos, c. 1600 BCE | [Bronze Age Crete](bronze-age-crete.jpg) | Clay, limestone, copper accents and fresco-blue sea |
| 2 | Chang'an and the Wei River valley, 750 CE | [Tang Chang'an](tang-changan.jpg) | Amber, cream and green glazed ceramic relief |
| 3 | Suruga Bay, Fuji and the Tōkaidō, 1833 | [Edo Suruga Bay](edo-suruga-bay.jpg) | Carved wood, layered paper and indigo printmaking colors |

## Design intent

The camera is close enough to reveal individual ridges, waterways, a settlement
landmark and a route. Story navigation, timeline, Sources, Meanwhile, Layers and
a World view affordance occupy consistent positions. Each region's material
treatment is an artistic interpretation informed by period craft.

These subjects explore broader story possibilities beyond the five v1 stories
in the [PRD](../../../PRD.md). Terrain, settlements, routes and inset artwork
are illustrative concept studies, not validated archaeological reconstructions
or production geographic data.

The [Living Diorama](../2026-09-25-explorations/living-diorama.jpg) was supplied
to the Bronze Age generation as a close-view composition reference. The resulting
Bronze Age screen was supplied to both Asian generations to retain the interface
and close-view layout while changing geography and materials.

A useful next experiment is the transition from the whole globe into these
regional material treatments, preserving navigation and geographical continuity.

## Historical and material references

The following museum and UNESCO sources informed the brief. They support the
historical context and material inspirations; the generated maps and artwork
are original visual interpretations.

- [Minoan Crete — The Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/de/essays/minoan-crete): Bronze Age Crete, palace centers, frescoes, trade and material crafts.
- [Greece: Minoans and Mycenaeans — British Museum](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/galleries/greece-minoans-and-mycenaeans): Aegean Bronze Age and Knossos material context.
- [Tracing the Development of Ceramics along the Silk Road — The Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/perspectives/ceramics-along-the-silk-road): Tang sancai glaze palette and ceramic material inspiration.
- [Silk Roads: Chang'an–Tianshan Corridor — UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1442): Chang'an and the historical route network.
- [Kusatsu, Fifty-three Stations of the Tōkaidō — The Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/36974): 1833–34 Edo woodblock print material and travel context.
- [Ōtsu, Fifty-Three Stations of the Tōkaidō — The Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/37248): Tōkaidō connecting Edo and Kyoto through post stations.
