# Wander: broader design explorations

Generated on 25 September 2026 with the built-in ImageGen tool.

The brief for this second set was to take substantial liberty with the globe,
materials, theme, and interface while pursuing an intuitive, magical, beautiful
history experience. These concepts use the [PRD](../../../PRD.md) for product goals.
They deliberately explore new visual systems beyond the original brass orrery.

The shared foundation is a globe that invites rotation and close exploration,
guided historical stories, a separate calendar timeline, concise narrative and
illustrations, access to sources, and a way to discover events elsewhere.
The concepts express interaction ideas; the images do not demonstrate working
interaction or performance. Geography, archival-style illustrations, and effects
are illustrative design studies.

## Design judgment

The Paper World is the strongest candidate for an approachable and distinctive
overall identity. The Living Diorama makes the most compelling close-up story
view. A refinement could explore the Paper World's material language through
the Diorama's camera and narrative composition, retaining a coherent globe
material while moving between scales.

The Luminous Atlas offers a strong alternative when nighttime atmosphere and
a quiet reading experience matter most.

## Exact generation prompts

Each was a separate new image generation from the product brief and the user's
request for broader creative freedom. No reference images were attached.

### The Luminous Atlas

```text
Use case: ui-mockup.
Create a breathtaking, production-quality desktop web-app concept for WANDER, an interactive history experience. The user explicitly asks for bold new creative directions: take substantial liberty with the globe, theme, materials, typography and arrangement. Use the specified new art direction confidently.
Target dimensions: 1600 x 1000, landscape 8:5. One single complete app screen, flat viewport, no device, browser chrome, perspective frame, collage or presentation board. Compose naturally for this exact aspect ratio.
Product goals to preserve: visitors immediately understand how to enter a historical story; a gorgeous interactive 3D globe remains the principal object; dragging rotates it, scrolling moves continuously from world to island; guided story beats connect places through time; a clearly readable calendar timeline can be scrubbed; a concise sourced narrative and one historical-style image support the selected beat; a quiet Meanwhile affordance leads to contemporaneous events elsewhere; Layers allows relief, ocean depths, borders, climate and events to be explored; users can return to their story after wandering. The launch stories include Tambora 1815–1816, Magellan–Elcano 1519–1522, The Century of Oil 1933–1974, The Black Death 1346–1353, and The California Gold Rush 1848–1855. This frame shows only one focused experience, not an inventory of the entire product.
Design a realistic, extremely beautiful, usable interface. Clear hierarchy, strong typography, purposeful imagery, generous spacing. Around 75% of the viewport belongs to the globe experience. One narrative grouping and an obvious primary action; secondary actions stay quiet. Rich visual craft belongs in the globe; surrounding controls should feel effortless and understandable.
Anchor reading sizes to roughly 16–18px and generous leading at the target size. Use at most two typefaces. Labels align precisely and stay readable. The timeline uses calendar dates, visible ticks, a clear current-date playhead, a play/pause control and sparse chapter pips. Keep time control separate from spatial rotation so their roles are immediately understandable.
No generic dashboard, metrics, login, account navigation, search box, dashboard cards, excessive tabs, conventional marketing hero, app-download button, floating browser window, random charts, fantasy kingdoms, steampunk ornament, gears, heavy brass surrounds or antique control panels. Do not just recolor an old globe design. This is a wholly new visual system.
All visible narrative dates are historical dates specified below, independent of today's date, 25 September 2026. Illustrative imagery is a concept study, without fabricated archive or photographic attribution. Geography should be recognizable and plausible, although sculptural relief is intentionally exaggerated. Make a complete design that could inspire a real WebGL application, with crisp graphic UI rather than pseudo-text.
Concept name: The Luminous Atlas.
Art direction: an intimate, almost sacred nocturnal observatory. A suspended, perfectly round Earth feels carved from pale luminous porcelain and submerged in deep translucent blue-green optical glass. Solid, sculpted ivory continents have extraordinary fine mountain relief; water is dark clear petrol glass, letting a few sculpted sea-floor ridges read below the surface. Very thin pearlescent atmosphere, fine warm amber light at historical events, deep midnight teal background. Restrained optical depth, real shadow and tactility, exceptionally fine detail. Evoke the wonder of a lit terrestrial lantern and the calm precision of a contemporary museum. The object is elegant and materially surprising. No metal armillary, pedestal, mechanical rings, decorative starfield, science-fiction HUD or holographic wireframe.
Composition: enormous complete globe occupying the center-left, 820px in diameter, centered approximately at x620 y450. Asia, the Indian Ocean and Southeast Asia face us; recognizable Africa near the left edge, Australia lower right. Tactile ivory relief and the translucent dark ocean form a beautiful light/dark contrast. Three subtle warm pinpricks of history, a stronger amber glow on Sumbawa, one fine leader to a tiny label "Tambora". Exaggerated Himalayas are beautiful readable sculpture. The globe floats within dark negative space, with a faint atmospheric halo and no table or physical room.
Story state: a Tambora story beat showing the global context of the 1815 eruption. Do not place a giant physical ash plume obscuring the global view: the amber event is enough at this scale.
Typography and interface: WANDER top left in a contemporary elegant humanist serif, much smaller than a billboard. A quiet "Stories" back affordance at the far left below it. Thin precise warm-white sans-serif labels and ivory editorial serif headline. At upper right only "Layers" and a sound icon.
At the right, an unboxed narrative grouping around 310px wide, on the same midnight background. Tiny amber date "10 APRIL 1815"; headline "A mountain changes the world"; body "Tambora erupts on Sumbawa. Follow the story from one island to a world of consequences." Below, one small framed monochrome engraving-style historical image crop in a simple thin ivory hairline, caption "Eruption study", and small "Read more · Sources". Main action a warm luminous cream rectangular button "Continue story" with a subtle arrow. Below that one unobtrusive line "Meanwhile, elsewhere ↗".
Bottom 110px of the screen: a beautifully quiet translucent midnight surface with a hairline timeline, white ticks, labels "1814" "1815" "1816" "1817"; a small luminous amber handle at 1815 and exact date "10 APR 1815" above it. A simple play triangle in a small circle on the left. A subtle second row of story beats shows eight tiny dots, one amber, but no charts or metrics. At the globe's bottom-left small instruction "Drag to turn · Scroll to get closer".
Magic should come from the illuminated material, scale, atmosphere, patient negative space and tiny lights that invite touch. The outcome is refined, dramatic, inviting and easy to understand.
```

Near the globe a single whisper of interaction guidance "Turn the world. Follow your curiosity." No heavy frames, busy navigation, ribbons or gears.
The astonishing paper-sculpted globe and airy editorial rhythm should make history feel welcoming, tactile, playful and thoughtful. The page feels like a beautiful physical atlas coming alive, with clear controls and enormous breathing room.
